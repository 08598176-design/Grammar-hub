# Upload guide — what to add to the repo, and how to scrub it first

For Andrew. You said you want to scrub files yourself rather than have a
model do it — good. This is the checklist, then the shopping list in
priority order. Files can trickle in; drop anything into `inbox/` and your
assistant will file it (see `inbox/README.md`).

## Scrub checklist (run per file, ~2 minutes each)

1. **Student names** — in the body, in headers/footers, in the filename.
   Marking sheets and feedback addressed to a named student are the highest
   risk. Class lists, marks, results: never upload.
2. **Student work** — scans, photos of books, handwriting, photos of
   students. Never upload, even anonymised; write a fresh teacher version if
   you want the exemplar. (Two PDFs already in the repo have student scans —
   see Q10 in `collab/QUESTIONS_FOR_ANDREW.md`; they should come out.)
3. **Document metadata** — Word: File → Info → Inspect Document → remove
   document properties and personal information. Low stakes for teacher names
   in a private repo, but it's the habit that matters.
4. **School-internal links** — SharePoint/Compass URLs often embed a
   `login_hint=` email. Strip or replace with a plain description.
5. **Keys and passwords** — never in a file. (The audio script's pattern —
   key passed on the command line, not stored — is the right one.)
6. **Copyright** — textbook page scans are tolerable colleague-sharing in a
   *private* repo but can never ship in anything deployed to a URL. When in
   doubt, paraphrase instead of scan. Check the repo says **Private** under
   Settings → General.

Rule of thumb: the repo should only ever contain things you'd be relaxed
about a stranger at another school reading.

## What to upload, in priority order

**P1 — unblocks work already queued:**
1. Your **latest local build** of the hub (the newest `vce-grammar-hub-test_*`
   folder, zipped, whole). The repo's copy is missing the Oral Exam section
   the audio belongs to. (Q1)
2. The **grammar checklist / sequence you actually teach from** per year
   level, however rough. The VCE study design PDF is already here; what's
   missing is *your* ordering and emphasis. (Q2, Q3)
3. Your answers to the questions file — via a chat with your assistant, no
   document needed.

**P2 — feeds the next builds:**
4. A rough **topic map**: year → term → topic → textbook chapter → key
   grammar points. Dot points in an email are fine. (Q6)
5. **One or two more topic folders** like Unit 10 (scrubbed), ideally the
   topic you're teaching next and one VCE Units 3&4 topic.
6. **Vocab lists per topic** — any format; a two-column word/meaning table is
   ideal, but your assistant can extract from worksheets.
7. **Oral exam / conversation question sets** beyond the 47 already here;
   past practice CATs (scrubbed) with their MODIFIED/INTERMEDIATE/ADVANCED
   tiers intact — the tiers are gold, they become the band structure.
8. **Rubrics and marking grids** you use for writing and orals.

**P3 — nice to have:**
9. **Teacher-written model answers** (WAGOLLs) for VCE writing forms — one
   per form is enough to start. No real student work, ever. (Q9)
10. A kanji **component family** you'd teach, as a worked example, if the
    kanji workbench gets a green light. (Q5)

## What NOT to upload

- Anything failing the scrub checklist above.
- Whole-textbook scans or publisher PDFs beyond incidental worksheet pages.
- Anything you'd have to think twice about — when unsure, describe the
  resource to your assistant instead, and it will tell you what's actually
  needed from it (often just the structure, not the document).
